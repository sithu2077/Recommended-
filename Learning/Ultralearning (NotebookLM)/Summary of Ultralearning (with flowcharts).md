### 5 minutes rule

The 5-minute rule is a method to overcome procrastination by committing to a task for just five minutes.

**The method is simple:**

1. Pick the task you're avoiding
    
2. Commit to working on it for ONLY 5 minutes
    
3. After 5 minutes, give yourself complete permission to stop
    
Here's why it works:

- The hardest part of any task is starting
    
- Five minutes feels psychologically "safe" - not a big commitment
    
- Once you begin, momentum often carries you forward
    
- Even if you do stop after 5 minutes, you've still made progress

## 4. Drill

- The direct-then-drill approach:
    - 1st step - practice skill directly.
    - 2nd step - analysis direct skill and isolate components that are difficult.
    - 3rd step - go back to direct skill and integrate what you've learned.

- Something mentally strenuous provides a greater benefit to learning than something easy.

## 6. Feedback

- The power of information.
- Feedback is an essential ingredient in reaching expert level.
- Lack of feedback can even result declining abilities.
- Feedback works well when it provides useful information that can guide failure learning.
- Feedback back fired when it is aimed at a person's ego (example, personal-praise or personal-criticism).

### The Feynman Technique

1. Write down concept/problem.
2. In space below, explain the idea:
    - if concept, explain it as simple as it can.
    - if problem, explain how to solve.
3. When stuck, go back to resources (e.g. text book, notes, ...) and find answers.

## 9. Experimentation

- The art of learning itself is a kind of trial and error.
- Have ***the growth mindset***.
- Experimentation is the key to ***mastery***.
- Find your own learning path/originality.
